Tor City Topographical & Morphological Study

Project Summary:

A comprehensive topographical and morphological study of Tor City, highly useful for urban planning, natural hazard evaluation (such as flood risks), and site selection for engineering projects and infrastructure networks based on ground characteristics[cite: 1].

1. Digital Elevation Model (DEM)

Concept: Illustrates elevation changes and depressions across the city and surrounding areas[cite: 1].

Map Key: Values range from (0.002 to 0.5) up to (2 to 10), showing terrain variation relative to sea level[cite: 1].

2. Slope Map

Concept: Computes terrain slope degrees to identify land gradients (flat, steep, mountainous)[cite: 1].

Map Key: Categories range from (1 to 20) up to (200 - 300) for highly rugged terrain areas[cite: 1].

3. Contour Map

Concept: Extracted contour lines representing topographic elevation equality[cite: 1].

Importance: Assists planning engineers in understanding surface shapes accurately and defining ridges and valleys[cite: 1].

4. Aspect Map

Concept: Shows the geographic direction that slopes face (North, South, East, West)[cite: 1].

Map Key: Flat (-1) for entirely level ground with precise directional angles[cite: 1].

Tor City Hydrological Study

Project Summary:

An advanced hydrological study of Tor City analyzing water flow behavior, flood paths, major and minor streams, essential for designing drainage networks and protecting infrastructure.

1. Water Flow Paths relative to Slope

Concept: Demonstrates flow accumulation and runoff directions based on terrain slope modeling.

Map Key: Color gradients show water concentration and flow intensity from high grounds toward drainage paths.

2. Major Streams in Tor City

Concept: Extraction and mapping of main valleys and major water streams within the city boundaries.

Importance: Defining core axes for drainage and flood risk assessment to secure vital facilities.

3. Stream Order Classification

Concept: Hydrological classification of stream networks based on stream orders (1 to 4).

Map Key: Gradation from first order (1) up to fourth order (4) with distinct colors to identify flow volume per channel.

by : Eng / Ammar Elsayed Morsi Mahmoud Arafat
